Surveillance platform Use case · zones + hours + dwell

The operator draws the fence. The pipeline never blinks.

Static zones trigger on shadows, animals and vehicles that are meant to be there. The system pairs GPU-side zone analytics with tracked detections, debounce and operating hours — zones drawn live by the operator, applied mid-session without a restart — and optionally asks the VLM to describe what crossed.

01The problem

"Someone is inside the restricted area" is an easy sentence and a hard classifier problem.

Raw motion detection inside a polygon fires on everything: light changes, parked machinery, passes by authorised traffic whose route touches the edge. And the geofence itself must be drawn by the people who know the site — meaning zone editing, versioning and immediate effect are product requirements, not nice extras.

Design target: an alert that names the zone, the object, and the reason — with debounce and hours doing the precision work a bare polygon can't.

02Zones as a live product

  • Drawn in the UI. A zone editor on the dashboard saves polygons per camera — normalized coordinates, selectable classes, and a mode per zone: restricted (alert on entry; crossing into it) and valid (alert on exit; crossing out of a place someone should not leave).
  • GPU-side analytics. The zone list drives a generated analytics config on the DeepStream sidecar; pipeline detection outputs debounced boundary-crossing events (zone id, object label, tracker id). Zone changes stream through: re-generating the config file triggers the live reload, so a mid-session redraw applies within seconds — no restart.
  • App resolution. The alert's zone membership is resolved fresh at the moment of the alert — an edited zone is never interpreted through stale geometry.

03Dwell variants

A second family of zones monitors staying, not crossing: vehicles that loiter in areas they shouldn't. The Python-side monitor tracks a vehicle's ground point (bottom-centre of the box) inside each polygon, counts dwell seconds, and fires once when the zone's threshold is crossed — with detector-dropout grace so a flickering box does not reset the clock, and a re-alert cooldown per stay. Alerts carry the measured dwell seconds against the threshold, and where a cross-camera global ID exists, the identity and plate come along.

05Reflection

The polygon is cheap. The semantics around it are the feature.

Two zones with identical geometry can mean the opposite ("alert when entered" vs "alert when left"), and the dwell variant exists because a stay is a different event to a crossing. Intrusion taught me that redeploying the same detection matrix under different geometry-aware semantics is what turns a detection library into behaviour.
