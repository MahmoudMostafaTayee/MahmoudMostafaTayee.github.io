Traffic analytics platform Use case · lane geometry + motion vectors

A wrong-way car must be told apart from one completing a turn.

Every lane carries a direction. The hard part is that "against the direction" is also exactly what a car looks like mid-turn, merging, or parking in reverse — so the evaluator needs motion sustained across frames inside a polygon it trusts, and the confidence to stay silent otherwise.

01The problem

Single-frame cues cannot separate wrong-way travel from turning, merging, or reversing.

A car pointing against the lane's flow occupies the same pixels as a car halfway through a U-turn. A bounding box carries no heading, and optical flow at CCTV frame rates is fragile under compression artifacts. The signal that actually distinguishes the cases is persistence: wrong-way travel keeps moving against the flow after a turn would have resolved, across enough frames that compression noise and detector jitter average out.

The second constraint was trust. A wrong-way alert with one false positive per shift becomes an alert operators read past. Precision mattered more than recall for this behaviour — a missed wrong-way vehicle is recoverable if the next camera sees it; a cadence of fake alerts is not recoverable.

Design target: a wrong-way event is only fired after sustained opposite motion inside a calibrated lane polygon — confirmed, evidenced, once per incident.

02The decision rule

Each lane carries a direction prior from calibration. The evaluator per track then does three checks:

  • Where. The track's ground point must sit inside the lane polygon — and stay there. Motion inside regions that don't fit the lane model (crossings, turn bays) is evaluated with the polygon excluded, so geometry decides who is eligible to be judged, not who is right.
  • How much. The motion vector, not the heading of the box, is compared against the direction prior — integrated over a sliding window of frames, not read from one instant. A car that crosses briefly against flow (a turn cutting into the lane, then back out) accumulates too little opposite travel to fire.
  • How long. The confirmation window is the main precision lever. Only after sustained opposite motion across a calibrated window does the alert fire; near turns, the window tolerates temporary misalignment, and a single reversed vector is never an event.

The evidence attached to the alert is the part operators actually use: the clip shows the vehicle entering the lane and continuing against the flow — a story, not a snapshot. That single property is what made alerts auditable instead of dismissible.

03Architecture

Wrong-way is one evaluator over the shared detection-and-tracking core; it reads track history and a per-camera lane config, and emits events.

Lane configuration is per camera and versioned with the site's setup, so a recalibration is a config change, not a code change — and the evaluator's output is testable against recorded track sequences before any camera is touched live.

04The hard parts

01

Intersections and turn bays

Real sites do not have clean one-direction corridors. The evaluator marks turn regions with looser geometry: opposite motion there contributes less evidence per frame, which effectively reserves alerts for sustained travel rather than turn-cutting.

02

Calibration rot

Lane polygons drift: the camera sags, the lens is replaced, the lane is repainted. The evaluator flags decaying evidence — candidate events that never confirm — as a calibration signal. Interval spikes of unconfirmable "like wrong-way" motion meant the polygon needed refreshing.

03

Multi-camera as a truth layer

One camera's wrong-way event can be cross-checked against the vehicle's next camera sighting. Where cross-camera tracking was available, the wrong-way alert attaches the vehicle's prior and next camera presence, so the operator reviews a journey — and misfires become visible as "wrong-way on one camera, normal arrival on the next".

05Reflection

Geometry decides who can be judged; time decides who fires.

Wrong-way is a narrow behaviour, but it taught the general lesson: a detector can't be handed rules — it needs a decision surface calibrated so well the threshold feels obvious. The confirmation window was a precision dial; per-camera calibration was an honesty mechanism. Most effort went to staying silent.
