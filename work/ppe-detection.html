Industrial safety monitoring Use case · detection + compliance policy

Compliance checked continuously — not by walking the floor.

Hard hats and vests are cheap to detect in a demo and hard to report honestly in production. The pipeline runs PPE inference on tracked person crops, smooths each item over time, and opens violations as episodes — one alert per unbroken unsafe stretch, with cooldowns and repeat-alert hygiene.

01The problem

Site staff could not be in every corner of the premises; the cameras already were.

The naive approach — run a PPE detector on the full camera frame and alert on misses — produces two failure modes. Distant people produce tiny heads the detector scores as "unknown", so full-frame inference left most answers unusable on CCTV-scale frames. And frame-level misses generate bursts of duplicate alerts for a single person's poorly-timed stance. Both are product failures disguised as model limitations.

Design target: a per-person verdict that is calm over time which items are missing, one alert per unsafe stretch, and "unknown" never firing.

02Crops, not frames

  • Two detectors, layered. A person detector with a tracker runs first; the PPE detector — separate classes for present and absent variants of each item, plus "unknown" — runs on each tracked person's crop. The plate is bigger in the crop; the helmet is too. Answer coverage jumped from mostly "unknown" under full-frame inference to usable per-person verdicts.
  • Crop rendering matters. Bilinear scaling at the DeepStream crop stage was required; nearest-neighbour scaling corrupted small-image statistics badly enough to yield almost no detections.
  • Association. Item boxes (hat region, torso region) are attached to tracked persons by geometry, so a violation is always about a person — an ID, a location in frame — not a floating box.

03Episodes, not frames

Per track and per item, a short memory defines state. An item counts as ABSENT only when seen absent in a supermajority of the last few evaluated frames — a handful-of-frames window — so a single flicker doesn't open anything. The moment the window says absent, a violation episode opens and the first alert fires once.

  • An episode closes only after a contiguous stretch of PRESENT readings — brief relief doesn't close it, so a person who ducks under the helmet twice in a minute is one episode.
  • "Unknown" readings (truncated, too small, occluded) never push the state in either direction. Missing evidence is not evidence of compliance or violation.
  • Per-item cooldowns and re-alert spacing mean the same missing helmet doesn't scream every evaluation.
  • A second missing item on the same person during an open episode is folded into the existing alert instead of doubling it.

Track loss caused the subtlest failures: tracker IDs recycle after a track ends, so a worker who leaves and returns legitimately looks like a new person. Geometric track linking (short gap, near centre distance) connects the chains, and a repeat alert is annotated "possibly the same person again" with the gap and distance evidence — grouping, honest, not suppression. An optional appearance-based grace exists but shipped off by default, because a wrong re-identification is worse than a duplicate alert.

Packaging was its own deliverable: the compose stack ships as a self-contained overlay — its own media source, sidecar preset, ports, and storage — with a distinct project name, so a site can switch it on per camera and run it beside any other stack on the same machine, with build, health-check, log, and teardown recipes a single verb away.

05The hard parts

01

Crowds and occlusion

Crowded, low camera angles stack workers; a crop carrying three helmets is meaningless. Persons too crowded or too small are skipped entirely rather than guessed — honest skipping beats wrong verdicts.

02

Evidence calibration

Item boxes from untracked detections are drawn on the live view as calibration evidence but never forwarded to alerts or VLM prompts — an internal signal is not an operational one.

03

Tuning on the aggregate

Per-site footage differs (vest colour schemes, helmet styles, winter clothing) so violation rates were reviewed per site before threshold changes; the model's "absent" classes are conservative by design, and the episode window is what tunes real-world alert volume without touching model weights.

05Reflection

The model detects hats. The episode logic detects non-compliance.

Safety reporting lives or dies on alert hygiene — one event per real stretch of unsafety, quiet otherwise. The engineering that mattered was temporal (windows, cooldowns, episode closure) and infrastructural (per-site packaging with exclusivity checks), not the detector itself.
