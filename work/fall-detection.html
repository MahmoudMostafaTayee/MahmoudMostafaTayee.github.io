Indoor safety · surveillance platform Use case · pose + temporal logic

One event per real fall. Not one alert per horizontal frame.

A fall is a transition, not a posture. Sitting, crouching, bending and collapsing all look horizontal in a single frame — so the detector is only the sensor, and the product lives in the state machine that watches the change over time.

01The problem

Single-frame detection cannot tell a fall from sitting down.

"A person is lying down" is a description of a pose; "a person fell" is a description of a change. The first is cheap — any detector can produce it — and also wrong most of the time: resting, cleaning, praying, stretching and sleeping all match it. The hard part was not detecting horizontal. It was distinguishing a fall from lying down, bending, and being occluded by furniture.

The failure modes that matter are symmetric: alerting on every reclined person buries the operator, and staying silent on a real collapse defeats the point of the camera. Both errors come from the same source — evaluating one frame instead of a transition.

Design target: every fall fires once, early enough to matter, with a clip window around the transition; ordinary postures never fire, no matter how horizontal they look.

02The signals

The evaluator reads three independent witnesses of the same event. Each one is individually fooled by something; together they narrow the space.

  • Pose keypoints. YOLOv8-Pose and MediaPipe give the body's geometry: torso vector, shoulder-to-hip angle, head position relative to the hips. Torso angle rotating from vertical toward horizontal is the cleanest single signature of a fall.
  • Bounding-box geometry. The detector's box aspect ratio flips from tall to wide, and the vertical centroid velocity spikes downward. This is the cheap fallback that still works when keypoints are missing or degenerate (from behind, at distance, or heavy occlusion).
  • Post-drop stillness. A fall ends in an abnormal state: the box stops moving where and how a standing person's box never would. Sustained stillness after the drop is what separates a fall from a dance move or a fast slide into a chair.
  • An action classifier as corroboration. A spatial-temporal GCN over keypoint windows classifies short action clips (walking, sitting, lying, instability, fall) in unit-normalized space. It is used as a supporting vote — never alone — because a classifier over 25 Hz resampled windows is slow to confirm and confident to be wrong; kinematics plus the classifier beats either witness by itself.

Everything is measured against a per-track rolling reference scale (a percentile of the box's recent geometry, outlier-clamped), so the same thresholds mean the same thing whether the person is five metres from the lens or 30.

03The state machine

The evaluator is a small, explicit state machine per track — not a classifier over frames.

Uprightbaseline posture
Transitionangle + aspect + downward v
Downlow centroid · horizontal torso
Stillsustained · no recovery
Eventone alert · clip window

The transition must be a transition: angle, aspect and velocity all moving together within a short window. The end state must be sustained: stillness is confirmed across frames before the event fires, which is what excludes sitting down or crouching — both cross the angle threshold briefly and then recover. Recovery at any point before confirmation resets the machine, silently. Controlled motions get explicit vetoes: small torso angle with the head still high is a bend, not a collapse; a deep knee bend inside a plausible sitting range is a sit, not a fall. And when a down candidate stands back up inside the confirmation window, a "recovered" outcome is recorded — valuable for tuning, deliberately not routed to alerts.

Thresholds are calibrated per camera. A ceiling view and a wall view disagree about what "horizontal" is; fitting thresholds per view — as part of the configuration, not as a preprocessing afterthought — was worth far more than any extra signal.

04Architecture

The fall evaluator is one behaviour over a shared detection-and-tracking substrate, side-effect free with respect to the tracker: it reads track history and emits events.

Because the evaluator is disconnected from live inference, it is reproducible: record the tracks, replay them, tune the confirmation window honestly against labelled event sequences. That replay loop is also how regressions were caught when the detector or pose model was swapped under the evaluator.

05The hard parts

01

Furniture and occlusion

A couch is a machine for generating false positives: people lower themselves into it slowly and end horizontal. Combining zone context and the recovery rule — a person who ends low but stands back up shortly after is sitting, not fallen — kept this manageable without hand-tuning for every floor plan.

02

Keyholes and distance

Small or partially visible people produce unusable keypoints. The box-geometry witness exists specifically for this: it is worse than pose when available and better than nothing when pose collapses, and the evaluator degrades from the strong witness to the weak one instead of going silent.

03

Latency vs. certainty

Confirming stillness costs exactly the seconds that matter in a real fall. The fix is staging: fire an early review state at the transition and a confirmed alert after stillness, so the alert always carries evidence instead of pretending certainty it does not have — while surfaces that care about speed can start looking at the review stage.

06Reflection

The model sees the pose; the state machine sees the fall.

Everything that made this useful was temporal: windowing, sustainment, recovery, cooldown. Those four words are where the product lived. The pose model was a commodity — the discipline of what counts as evidence was not.

If I rebuilt it, I would record per-track decision traces (what each witness said, frame by frame) as a first-class output from day one. Debugging "why did/didn't it fire?" from decision traces is minutes; from raw video it is an afternoon.
