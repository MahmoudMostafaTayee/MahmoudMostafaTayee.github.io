On-site surveillance platform Use case · Re-ID + topology + VLM arbitration

One global ID per vehicle, no matter how many cameras it crosses.

Track IDs reset at every camera boundary, duplicate when one vehicle enters two views, and lie when an occlusion ends a track. The system pairs per-camera tracklets with appearance embeddings, gates candidates with a site topology and travel time, and asks a vision-language model to judge the merges the numbers cannot settle.

01The problem

Three failure modes: IDs reset, IDs duplicate, IDs lie.

Every camera runs its own tracker, so identity is local by construction. A vehicle seen by three cameras becomes three IDs. A vehicle that pauses for two minutes returns with a new ID — and a visit with no start or end. Worse, distant or partially-occluded views make the tracker split one vehicle into two boxes moving together.

Naive fixes fail in production. Merging on nearest-in-time merges everything near a shared edge. Merging on appearance alone is genuinely weak for box-trucks and vans, which look identical cross-camera — measured on real footage, same-vehicle crops produce similarity distributions that overlap the negatives by a lot. The honest number: cross-camera true pairs and false pairs in the published vehicle-ReID world sit around 0.54 vs 0.36 mean similarity — a real signal, but not a line you can draw once and ship.

Design target: never merge when in doubt; judge doubtful merges explicitly; never split one vehicle inside one camera; never merge one vehicle with itself.

02Per-camera layer

  • Detection. A single-class vehicle detector fine-tuned for the site's geometry, exported through ONNX and running as TensorRT FP16 (INT8 for capacity) inside a batched DeepStream pipeline — ~15 concurrent 1440p streams on one consumer-class GPU with the detector on every 2nd–3rd frame.
  • Stabilizing before identity. A box stabilizer holds detections across detector flicker and drops nested/part boxes, and a lightweight IoU tracker produces the internal per-camera tracklets — deliberately decoupled from the platform tracker whose IDs proved unstable on distant views.
  • Crops. Tracklets accumulate representative vehicle crops asynchronously (strided, size-gated, batch-embedded) instead of embedding every frame.
  • Direction. At entrance cameras, a VLM verdict on the vehicle's end-orientation versus camera geometry decides arriving/leaving — only confirmed arrivals open a new visit; a leaving sight closes one.

03Cross-camera association

Association is gate then score then judge. The gates are cheap and physical; the score is the appearance signal; the judge handles the residual ambiguity.

  • Entrance rule. Only vehicles first seen at designated entrance cameras, confirmed arriving, may open a global track. Every other camera may only join an existing one. That single rule eliminates a whole class of duplicated identities.
  • Topology gate. The site is declared as a graph: overlapping-view groups and adjacency edges with minimum travel times. Unconnected groups can never hand off; adjacent pairs gate on minimum disappearance time; same-camera re-entry (occlusion regain) has its own window.
  • Timing gates. A max-absence TTL closes stale tracks; an exited track can never be a candidate again — a returning vehicle is a new visit, not a resurrection.
  • Two-zone scoring. Cosine similarity against each candidate's per-camera and combined mean embeddings falls into three outcomes: below a low gate → never merge; above a high match threshold → merge; in between → the ambiguous zone, which is never auto-merged. "Don't gamble on a merge" is the explicit principle — that zone exists for the arbiter.
  • Cluster constraint. Union-Find over tracklets with one invariant: a cluster may never hold two tracklets simultaneously on the same camera, so one vehicle can never grow two simultaneous IDs in a single view.

04VLM arbitration

With the arbiter enabled, topology and timing become eligibility filters only — they say who it could be, never who it is. For every new tracklet, up to a few ranked candidates (by Re-ID similarity and temporal closeness) get their per-camera, time-spread crops compared side by side by a small Qwen3-VL-class model at temperature 0, answering structured same / different / uncertain with matched and conflicting traits. The prompt encodes multi-camera commonsense: "not visible" is not a contradiction, apparent size is not identity, partial crops are allowed — and uncertain is preferred over forcing a decision.

Latency engineering mattered as much as prompt quality. Greedy decoding occasionally looped until the context cap, and shared-GPU contention could stretch one call by orders of magnitude. Hard token caps, a concise output instruction, image shrink-retries, worker pools, and a per-request wall-clock timeout got arbitration to roughly four seconds per candidate pair in the normal case — and any track that stays unresolved past the wait cap simply starts as a new track. Unresolved is preferred over wrong.

Every arbitration is logged — crops, exact prompt, verdict, latency — so "why didn't this merge?" is answerable from records, not re-runs.

Outputs: one stable global ID with per-camera aliases and visit history, published in every frame's metadata; a best-shot gallery (one quality-scored crop per camera per vehicle); per-vehicle plate readings; and journey events — sightings, transitions, travel times — consumed by the operator dashboard. A global ID is never rewritten once assigned.

Benchmarked on a public academic dataset with the official IDF1 protocol. The gap between the ground-truth-detection and real-detection runs is honest and diagnostic: detection over-production, not association, dominated the loss — which re-aimed the optimization work at detection cleanup.

Two measured negative results shaped the design. An opt-in homography spatial model — ground-plane projection as "spatial proof" — was instrumented and found net-negative: of 28 merges it made on opposite-side camera pairs, 27 were wrong vehicles, because bottom-centre projections are orientation-biased (front vs rear of the vehicle). It was demoted to opt-in for verified tight pairs only. Meanwhile the shipped Re-ID encoder was an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50, not a formally vehicle-trained one — the benchmark harness on real site footage showed it beat the domain-trained model handily, so domain training lost to measurement.

07Reflection

Topology decides who could be who; appearance is an opinion; the VLM is a judge, not an oracle.

The system's quality came from refusing to optimize one number. Comparator thresholds, a candidate cap, an uncertain verdict, an unresolved fallback, and an invariant cluster rule each trade a little recall for a lot of trust. A false merge is silent — it corrupts every event downstream for that vehicle — which is why the whole preference order exists.

The next iteration is methodological: a record/replay harness that logs every eligible candidate pair (not just the greedy pick) at full pipeline speed, then replays verdicts offline and re-clusters under swappable policies against a human-verified gold mapping. Live latency under GPU contention conflates "the model isn't good enough" with "the model never got GPU time"; replay separates them.
