Parking + site management Use case · detection on crops + consensus OCR

Find one vehicle in a full lot — and know how long it has been there.

Walking the floor to find a vehicle is the last manual step in a system that tracks everything. The answer: detect plates on vehicle crops, hold them positionally steady inside the vehicle's box across frames, read with a fine-tuned Arabic-script OCR served off the GPU's critical path, and publish one consensus read per cross-camera vehicle — searchable by time, bay and duration parked.

01The problem

Plates are tiny, beautiful targets for every failure mode of a detector.

In a full CCTV frame a plate is a handful of pixels tall: full-frame detection found roughly a third of plates, and any reading attempted at that resolution invents characters instead of reading them. The reading must also be operationally correct — a system that confidently prints a wrong plate damages trust more than one that honestly reads nothing half the time.

Design target: never read from a box too small to contain real characters, never show a plate that flickers between candidates, and derive one headline reading per vehicle from everything the fleet of cameras saw.

02Crops and holds

  • Secondary stage on crops. The plate detector runs inside each tracked vehicle's crop, rescaled to 640 — the plate is several times larger in a crop than in the full frame, and the inference is cheaper. Measured: plates found on ~53% of crops at 640 vs ~34% of full frames.
  • A real plate has physics. Size floors, aspect-ratio bands, not touching the frame edge, inside its parent vehicle's box, low and central (a plate doesn't ride the roof), one plate per vehicle chosen with stickiness — shown boxes are continuous, so the plate never teleports between two candidates frame to frame.
  • Vehicle-relative holds. The detector re-runs periodically, but between detections the plate's position is remembered relative to the vehicle's box, smoothed, and re-drawn on the current box — so it moves with the vehicle instead of blinking on and off.
  • Every rejection is logged. Once a vehicle leaves view, a diagnostic records whether a plate was kept and why each candidate was dropped — an audit trail for tuning precision without guessing.

03The reader

  • Data from the real pipeline. Training crops were harvested by the live system itself — one crop per track per day, manually reviewed — combined with an academic regional-plate dataset as generic filler. The test split is held out by camera session: the most recent capture per camera for test, the second for validation. Frames adjacent to training data never decide the benchmark.
  • Model. A mobile OCR recognition head fine-tuned at high input resolution into a compact digit-plus-Arabic-letter alphabet, CTC-decoded, exported to ONNX and served on CPU with a single thread — the GPU pipeline never competes with the reader.
  • Sharpness-gated enhancement. Sharpening (upscale + local contrast + unsharp, exactly matching the fine-tuning preprocessing) is applied only when the crop is already crisp by measurable sharpness statistics; blurry crops are enlarged and left alone, because sharpening blur creates artifact characters.

One training detail mattered disproportionately: padding had to be mid-grey after normalisation, per the reference implementation's resize semantics. Black padding silently taught the model to read digits only — a whole class of half-reads traced back to that single preprocessing constant.

04Consensus

A background bookkeeper keeps, for each cross-camera vehicle ID, the best crop of each camera with its reading. Consensus then works like an election with structural rules:

Cropsbest per camera · per vehicle
Digitsconfidence-mass vote
Lettersslot count → slot alignment
Gatedigit run required · low-confidence withheld
Publishheadline read + per-camera evidence

Digit runs resolve to the string with the most confidence mass behind it. Letters are harder: first how many letter slots exist (confidence-share weighted), then a slot-alignment step that tolerates dropped letters and substitutions so one missed letter doesn't shift the entire sequence. Readings below the floor stay attached to their crops as evidence but never enter the headline field — the UI shows the level of certainty it actually has.

05Architecture

Plate objects publish alongside vehicles in frame metadata; the cross-camera vehicle ID comes from the Re-ID layer (embeddings as a cheap rejection gate, with VLM arbitration for hard merges), and the app folds per-vehicle JSON into one vehicle feed: consensus reading, per-camera crops with readings, entry and exit timing, duration parked. The bookkeeper never blocks the frame pipeline — better crops always arrive again.

The recognition quality is reviewed end-to-end: batch tooling emits per-crop CSV/JSON with text and confidence for eyeballing, and detector candidates are compared on the held-out split with per-camera breakdowns before any model change ships.

06Reflection

Consensus turned a flaky string into a reliable field.

A single OCR read is a measurement, not a fact. Treating every read as evidence with confidence mass — and structuring the alphabet's known regularities (digit-run length, letter slots) into the vote — is what made the headline field trustworthy enough to search on. The second lesson was pipeline topology: bigger crops beat bigger frames, and holding coordinates relative to the moving vehicle beat re-detecting blindly.
