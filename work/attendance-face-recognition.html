Identity platform Use case · alignment + gallery matching + derived sessions

Attendance from reality: sightings in, sessions derived.

Hand-compiled rosters are slow to build and trivially gameable. The pipeline detects faces attached to tracked people, aligns and embeds them, stabilises identity with a voting window, coalesces recognitions into one sighting fact per person per place per minute — and derives attendance (breaks, lateness, absence) from those facts in a policy pass. Sessions are never stored; they are recomputed.

01The problem

Recognition is easy; stability is the product.

A face matched once per minute produces a timeline of flickering labels and double-counted people. Every stage had to respect the same reality: faces are small and tilted on CCTV, tracker IDs are unreliable carriers of identity, and an attendance sheet that says a person left their desk for a ten-second break is a system nobody believes.

Design target: names that stick once earned and are hard to displace; one sighting fact per person per place per minute; attendance as a pure derivation over facts.

02Recognition quality

  • Ladder of models. Person detection and body tracking first, then a compact face detector as a secondary stage on each tracked person — a face never exists without a person context — then embedding and gallery match.
  • Alignment is the multiplier. Five-point landmarks straighten every face into the canonical crop before embedding. Matching tilted live crops against upright enrollment photos measured a similarity gap of ~0.15–0.2 versus ~0.35–0.4 aligned — alignment mattered more than the embedding model.
  • Floor filters. Minimum face size and confidence gates stop degraded faces from reaching the recognizer at all; a wrong ID is worse than no ID.
  • Open-set guard. The best gallery identity must beat the runner-up by a real margin or the face stays unknown, and temporal voting over a sliding window makes a label stick: minimum votes to earn it, a supermajority to displace it, a re-earn rule when the face stops matching, and a spatial-jump guard for recycled tracker IDs.

03Facts → attendance

Recognitions are coalesced into one Sighting row per person, camera and minute bucket before storage. All higher-order state — sessions, breaks, lateness — is recomputed from sightings, never stored:

Gap semantics are the policy: sub-five-minute gaps are noise, five-to-thirty minutes become breaks, longer closes the session — with break-area cameras exempted and a simultaneous office sighting winning over a break-area one. Work hours, grace minutes and camera roles come from a small policy file, so changing the rules never means rewriting the recognition. Bucketing is deliberately decoupled from video timestamps so a looping replay can't poison wall-clock arithmetic.

Growth path: unknown faces above size/confidence floors are kept as candidate crops (novelty-filtered) and surfaced in a gallery page for an operator to name — human-in-the-loop enrollment that expands coverage from live footage instead of staged photo days.

04Architecture

Three cooperating pieces, connected by a message queue — the recognition sidecar (GStreamer/DeepStream, TensorRT), an independent attendance service, and a React frontend. A single adapter translates the wire format: an anti-corruption layer that kept the streaming side and the business side free to evolve (and break) independently.

Detections, annotated JPEGs and trim signals flow over bounded streams; the attendance service persists sighting facts in SQLite and runs REST and WebSocket APIs. The live page renders the current attendance table from full-state snapshots per flush — simple, atomic, and resilient to churn — alongside per-camera video tiles with rescaled overlay timestamps.

The crop path is also the correct path: re-detecting faces inside already-tracked bodies both cost latency and could silently miss a face the first pass had found. Reusing the tracked crop removed a whole failure class, not just milliseconds.

06Reflection

Facts in, views derived — the same discipline as event sourcing, applied to a camera feed.

Storing sightings and deriving everything else made policy changes free and history honest. The honest gap: enhancement and filtering are not liveness. A printed photo is handled by size/quality rules and temporal consistency, not by a dedicated anti-spoofing detector — that is the first thing I would bolt on if this went into an uncontrolled space.
